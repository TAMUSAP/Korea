Why Korea?

The K-Wave didn't happen by accident. People made it happen.

In about 70 years, Korea went from a war-torn country to a home of global brands, chart-topping music and food you can find in any grocery store. The secret is how Korea invests in its people. This summer, you'll see it up close.

1953The Korean War ends. You'll stand at the DMZ where it was divided.
TodayKorean companies and K-pop reach the whole world. You'll go inside both.

      FAQ

      Got questions? Good.

      I've never been abroad. Is that okay?

      Absolutely. Many students on faculty-led programs are first-time travelers. Your faculty and API's local staff are with you the whole time, with 24/7 support.

      Do I need to speak Korean?

      No. Zero Korean required. All program activities, including company and university visits, are conducted in English. Signs in Seoul are in English too, and your guides and faculty speak both languages.

      Are there scholarships or financial aid?

      Yes. Contact the Study Abroad office and meet with an advisor, who can help you identify funding opportunities: Meet with an advisor. You can also browse funding options here: Ways to fund study abroad.

      Who can apply?

      Texas A&M undergraduate students (EHRD 489) and graduate students (EHRD 689). The course is 3 credits in Summer I.

      Who will travel with us?

      Two Texas A&M faculty members and three local guides will be with the group throughout the program.

      Do I need a passport?

      Yes, and don't forget to start now. You can apply on campus through Texas A&M. It takes about 4–6 weeks and costs about $130. TAMU Passport Services

      Is the DMZ safe?

      Yes. You'll visit on an official guided tour, the way thousands of visitors do every year.

      What's the weather like in May?

      Warm and pleasant, with daytime highs in the low-to-mid 70s °F and cooler evenings. Pack comfortable walking shoes, a light jacket, a small umbrella and one business-casual outfit for company visits.

      How will I use my phone?

      The easiest option is an eSIM with a Korean data plan, which you can set up before you leave. Free Wi-Fi is also everywhere in Seoul, including hotels, cafés and the subway.

      Will I have a roommate?

      Yes. Students share hotel rooms with classmates from the program. It's one of the fastest ways to make friends.

      I'm vegetarian or have food allergies. Can I join?

      Yes. Share your dietary needs when you apply so we can plan group meals. Many Korean dishes use meat or seafood broth, so we'll help you prepare.

      Will I have free time?

      Yes. You get a free day and free afternoons to explore Seoul your way.

      Is airfare included?

      No. Faculty will share flight tips and the arrival time to aim for.
